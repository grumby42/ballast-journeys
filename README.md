# Ballast

Real balloon journeys, flown again. One offline HTML file: open `index.html` in any browser, phone or desktop. No network calls; progress saves in your browser.

You take the basket (and sometimes the ground crew's place) on the actual day, with the real balloon's size, gas and ballast. As you fly, the real records arrive: the pilots' own logs, letters from people watching, families waiting for news. Keep your own log; at the end it sits beside theirs.

## Journeys

| Journey | Year | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| First lift | 1783 | Charles and Robert's hydrogen balloon from the Tuileries: sand and the valve, the second sunset, what happens when weight leaves the basket |
| The cold ascent | 1862 | Glaisher and Coxwell's high flight: the standard atmosphere, hypoxia, setting a turn-back point before you need it |
| The Eagle | 1897 | Andrée's Arctic flight and the ice: margins, drag ropes and the ceiling, walking against a drifting floe |

## How the record is used

- Every story beat is labelled **documented**, **inferred** or **simplified**.
- Entries from the record are short digests in the game's own words, with sources.
- Direct quotations come only from texts published before 1929, in their original language with a translation. Modern translations and modern books are never quoted.
- The physics is real (standard atmosphere, real gas densities, venting at the ceiling, drag ropes). Where the record is silent (sand aboard, some masses, winds on unrecorded days) the game says what it assumed.

Each mission is scored against an expert flying the same seed: 1000 means you matched the expert.

## Sources

Listed in the game's Record tab for each journey: Charles's 1783 account, Benjamin Franklin's letters to Joseph Banks, Glaisher's reports and *Travels in the Air*, Coxwell's memoir, *Med Örnen mot polen* (1930), Lachambre and Machuron (1898), and others.
